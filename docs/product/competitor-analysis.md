## The short answer

No single thing Adray Core does is unique; the combination, the market and the team are. Every capability has a direct competitor, several of them well funded and cheaper.

- **Attribution** is crowded: Triple Whale, Northbeam, Polar, Hyros, Cometly, wetracked and a dozen smaller tools all pitch "see what's really driving revenue, not what Meta claims."
- **"AI-native" is no longer a differentiator.** Triple Whale ships an MCP connector for Claude and ChatGPT plus its own AI agent (Moby); Polar sells a "Headless MCP"; Windsor.ai and Amplitude include MCP access even on their free plans.
- **Behavior analysis** competes with free tools: Microsoft Clarity is free and has AI session summaries; Amplitude and FullStory cover it at enterprise scale, and Amplitude now ships AI agents that recommend next steps.
- **Forecasting** exists in Lebesgue (SMB, from $59/mo) and Prescient AI (enterprise MMM).

**Where Adray can be genuinely different:**

1. **The whole business in one answer.** Attribution, site behavior, products, inventory and a forecast together, tied to "what to do next." Competitors each own one or two of these.
2. **Built for our focus market (internal only, never claimed in messaging).** Spanish-first, peso budgets, VTEX, WhatsApp and (later) Mercado Libre. Nearly every competitor is Shopify-first, US or European, and English-first.
3. **A real team included.** Competitors sell software with support; Adray sells software plus a monthly review and custom work.
4. **Ecommerce and lead gen in one product.** Competitors pick one: Triple Whale and Polar for stores, Hyros and Cometly for funnels and B2B.

**What no one owns yet:** Triple Whale already automates morning reports (Moby Automations), so a daily summary alone isn't unique. No competitor tells a manager where to push their team, based on verified sales, with a real team behind it. That's the position to own: *Know where to push your team.*

**Recommendation in one line:** be familiar in category, different in promise. Use the words buyers already search for (ecommerce analytics, attribution, true ROAS), then win on "know where to push your team," verified sales, and the team behind it.

**The real competitor in LatAm is often not software.** It's the agency's monthly report, a GA4 dashboard, or a spreadsheet the Head of Ecommerce builds. No LatAm-native attribution platform of note turned up in this research.

## Pricing across the market

The market splits into three bands: under $300/mo for small stores, $700–$1,700/mo for mid-market, and $1,500+/mo for premium measurement; Adray's $625/mo sits at the bottom of the middle band while bundling more service than anyone.

| Polar Analytics | $720/mo under $5M GMV; ~$1,660/mo at $10–15M; add-ons extra | GMV, modular | No; demo first |
| Northbeam | $1,500/mo Starter (some report $1,000); Professional from ~$2,500 | Ad spend and pageviews | No |

| Microsoft Clarity | Free | — | Free |

Prices come from third-party trackers and change often; confirm on each vendor's page before quoting.

**What the pricing tells us:**

- **Revenue (GMV) is the standard value metric** in ecommerce analytics. Adray prices by sessions (decided Oct 2026): less common, but defensible, since sessions are what Adray tracks, they fit lead gen as well as ecommerce, and Northbeam's entry tier is also traffic-based. Show a revenue equivalent so buyers can compare.
- **The bottom is being pushed toward free** (Triple Whale's cut, Clarity, Lebesgue's free plan). Competing on attribution price alone is a losing game.
- **Mid-market buyers already pay $700–$1,700/mo** for Polar-style profit analytics without a human team. Adray's Growth offer at 15,000 MXN/month (about $850) with a team sits at the low end of that range.
- **Add-ons are how competitors really earn.** Polar and Triple Whale sell incrementality, audiences and AI agents on top. Adray bundling everything is simpler, but may leave money on the table.

## Where Adray overlaps and where it's different

"What's working" is a crowded promise, "what's making money" is contested by Polar, and "what to do next" is the most open ground.

| Promise | Who else claims it | How crowded | Adray's edge | How to prove it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **What's working** (attribution + behavior) | Triple Whale, Polar, Northbeam, Hyros, Cometly; wetracked for tracking; Clarity, Amplitude and FullStory for behavior | Very | Attribution and site behavior in the same answer; works on VTEX, WooCommerce and custom sites, not just Shopify | Side-by-side platform vs real results, plus one behavior finding with a peso value |
| **What's making money** (revenue, margin, products) | Polar, Triple Whale, Lebesgue | Moderate | Checked against real orders or closed CRM deals, including lead gen | Margin by channel and top products, matching the finance team's numbers |
| **What to do next** (forecast, actions, memory) | Lebesgue (SMB), Prescient (enterprise MMM), Triple Whale's Moby | Low in mid-market | Forecast plus inventory plus memory of what worked, reviewed with a real team every month | A forecast that lands close to the month's close; actions that worked last time |
| **In the tools you already use** | Triple Whale MCP, Polar MCP, Amplitude MCP, Windsor.ai, Supermetrics | Growing fast | Table stakes now, not a differentiator | Mention as a feature, not a headline |
| **Focus market (internal)** | Nobody strong | Open | Spanish-first, MXN, VTEX, WhatsApp, local logos | LG, Juguetrón, UVM case studies in Spanish |
| **A real team** | Agencies (human only); Northbeam's media strategist at higher tiers | Low in software | Software plus monthly review plus custom work, at a mid-market price | Named contact, monthly review ritual |

## Inline or different?

Both, at different layers: stay inline with the category so buyers can find and trust Adray, and be clearly different on the promise, the market and the delivery.

| Layer | Inline or different | Why | What it means for the copy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Category** | Inline | Buyers search "attribution", "true ROAS", "Triple Whale alternative". A made-up category makes them do the work | Say "attribution platform" early on the page and in SEO; keep comparison pages (vs Triple Whale, vs Polar, vs Northbeam) |
| **Promise** | Different | "See what's really driving revenue" is everyone's headline. "What to do next" is not | Lead with the three-part promise; make "what to do next" the part that stands out |
| **Market** | Different | No strong LatAm-native competitor; incumbents are English-first and Shopify-first | Internal targeting only. Externally: a Spanish site version, local currency, integrations list and client logos; never claim a region |
| **Delivery** | Different | Competitors sell software; agencies sell people. Adray can sell both | "Software plus a team that reviews it with you every month" |
| **AI / MCP** | Inline | Triple Whale and Polar both have it; claiming it as unique invites an easy rebuttal | Present as "works in ChatGPT and Claude", not as the headline |
| **Price** | Inline on structure, different on value | Buyers expect GMV-based tiers and a cheap entry; they don't expect a team at mid-market prices | Keep session-based tiers (decided) with a revenue equivalent shown; anchor Growth against Polar and Northbeam, not Triple Whale |

**Three changes for the current site, based on this:**

1. Replace "How is this different from Triple Whale?" answers that lean on "no new dashboard" and "works in your AI"; Triple Whale now does both. Lead with the banner: verified sales, five moves with owners, and a real team.
2. Add a Polar comparison page; it's the closest match to "what's working and what's making money."
3. Stop leading with "AI-native" and "portable everywhere"; lead with "Know where to push your team."

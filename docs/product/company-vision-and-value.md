## Adray Core: position and banner

**Position (what we own):** Adray Core is the AI-powered performance team for ecommerce brands. *You + Adray = your performance team.*

**Category (so buyers can compare us):** ecommerce analytics and attribution, for performance marketing teams. Lead gen: marketing attribution for lead-gen teams.

**Banner:**

> **Know where to push your team.** Every morning, Adray Core checks your ads, store and inventory against real sales, and tells you the five moves that matter most, with a team that helps you make them.

Spanish: **Sabe exactamente dónde enfocar a tu equipo.** *Cada mañana, Adray revisa tus anuncios, tu tienda y tu inventario contra tus ventas reales, y te dice las cinco acciones que más importan, con un equipo que te ayuda a ejecutarlas.*

**Three proof points:**

1. **Real sales, not platform claims.** Every number checked against your orders or closed deals.
2. **Five moves every morning.** Ranked by money at stake, each with an owner on your team.
3. **A team behind it.** People who review your priorities with you, and build AI that takes routine work off your team's plate.

**Where it came from:** a client told us, "I don't need to see all the data, I need to know how to push my team and where." Test it with three more clients before launch.

**Hierarchy for all copy:** position and banner first; the three promises (what's working, what's making money, what to do next) as the explanation; capabilities as proof.

## Adray Core: the daily story

Every morning, Adray tells each customer how their business, marketing and technology did, in one line short enough to read as a text message. It's the reason to open Adray every day, and the tip of everything underneath.

**What a good daily line sounds like:**

- *Normal day:* "On track for $1.2M, 6% below target. Meta is claiming 38 sales it didn't drive. Two bestsellers run out Thursday."
- *Good day:* "Best Tuesday this quarter: $48k, up 22%. Google Shopping did the heavy lifting. Nothing needs you today."
- *Lead gen:* "31 deals in the pipeline from last week's ads. Campaign B brings leads that never close; move budget to Campaign A."

**Rules for every line:**

1. One number against a goal.
2. One surprise they wouldn't have spotted alone.
3. One action, or permission to relax: "Nothing needs you today" builds trust.
4. Pesos and plain words, in the customer's language.
5. Never longer than a text message (about 140 characters).

**Where it shows up:** as the email subject line, so the value arrives without opening it; in Slack or WhatsApp at the same time every morning; and at the top of the welcome screen. One tap from any of them opens the full story.

**The welcome screen beneath it:**

1. **The story in one line.**
2. **Three health checks:** Business (sales vs target, margin, forecast), Marketing (real return on ad spend and customer cost), Technology (site speed, tracking health, connections, checkout errors).
3. **What changed:** the few movements that matter since yesterday or last week, each with a plain-language why.
4. **Five things to do:** ranked by pesos at stake, each with the reason and buttons to do, assign or dismiss. Adray tracks what was done and what happened next, so tomorrow's five get better.
5. **Below the waterline:** links into attribution, behavior, forecast and dashboards for anyone who wants detail.

Today's daily Signal PDF becomes optional detail behind the line, not the headline.

## Adray Core positioning: you + Adray = your performance marketing team

The daily report as a format isn't unique — Triple Whale's Moby already automates morning reports and media-buying actions — but what's behind Adray's line can be: verified sales, the whole business, five ranked actions, and a real team.

|  | What competitors offer | What Adray adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daily report | [Triple Whale Moby Automations](https://triplewhale.com/product-updates); generic AI morning briefs | Numbers checked against real orders and deals, not platform claims |
| Scope | Ads and store metrics | Business, marketing and technology in one line |
| Actions | Moby takes media-buying actions | Five ranked actions across the business, with results tracked |
| Who's behind it | An AI teammate (Triple Whale's pitch for Moby) | A team: software, AI and real people who review it with you |

**The line, tuned by audience:**

| Audience | Line | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founder / small brand | *You + Adray = a full performance marketing team.* | They don't have one and can't afford to hire it |
| Head of Ecommerce | *Your team, with an analyst, a strategist and AI on call every morning.* | They have a team; Adray makes it bigger |
| Performance marketer | *Spend your time on decisions, not reports.* | The champion must feel helped, never replaced |
| Agency | *Every client's daily story, before they ask.* | Makes the agency look sharper to its clients |

**Always "you + Adray", never "instead of".** Telling a performance marketer or an agency that Adray replaces them turns the champion into a blocker.

**Using the daily story in marketing:**

- Put real (anonymized) daily lines in the home page hero; the product explains itself.
- Offer *"Get your **free diagnostic**"* next to "Book a demo" as the entry point; the daily story starts during onboarding.
- Open every sales demo with the prospect's own first daily story.
- Post anonymized lines weekly on social as proof of what Adray notices.

## Adray Core value proposition, deeper

The five capabilities in the CEO pitch are not five products: three are the promises, and two are the foundation that makes the promises possible.

| Promise | Capability | What it does, in plain words | Ecommerce | Lead gen |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What's working | **Attribution** | Follows each customer from first click to verified purchase or closed deal, then credits the right ads, campaigns and channels | Anchored on the store order | Anchored on the CRM deal, not the form |
| What's working | **Behavior analysis** | Reviews every site visit with AI: where people click, get stuck, and what separates buyers from bouncers | Conversion rate, AOV, LTV drivers | Form and landing page friction |
| What's making money | **Attribution + margin and inventory data** | Shows revenue and margin by channel, campaign and product, not just ROAS | Which SKUs carry the store | Cost per closed deal |
| What to do next | **Forecasting ("How much will you sell this month?")** | Uses 2–5 years of daily data plus inventory to project the month and flag risks as it unfolds | Month close, stock-out impact | Pipeline and deal forecast |
| What to do next | **Cortex (marketing memory)** | Records every significant change across connected accounts, why it happened and its impact, so advice builds on what worked before | Seasonal learnings, past promos | What campaigns filled the pipeline |
| Foundation | **Unified data layer + Mark + MCP** | Connects Meta, Google, GA4, store, CRM, email and app analytics into one place you can ask in plain language, in Adray or in Claude/ChatGPT | All | All |

**How to use this in copy:** headlines speak in promises; body copy names the capability as proof; the foundation shows up as "works with the tools you already use." Cortex and the data layer are strong in decks and for technical buyers, but rarely belong in a headline.

## Language guide

Say it the way the customer says it: sales, money, customers, next month. Save the technical words for proof.

| Use | Instead of | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| real sales, verified sales | reconciled attribution, verified conversion | Customers think in orders and deals |
| what's working | multi-touch attribution | Name the outcome; the method is proof |
| where people get stuck | UX/UI friction, session analysis | Concrete and visual |
| how your month will close | predictive modeling | It's the question they already ask |
| remembers what worked | persistent marketing memory graph | Cortex is the name, memory is the benefit |
| in ChatGPT or Claude | MCP, data layer | Most buyers don't know MCP |
| a real team | service layer, white-glove | Warmer and specific |

**Product names:** "Adray" is the company; "Adray Core" is the product. Inside Core, Mark, Signal and Cortex are feature names a new buyer shouldn't need to learn first; use them in the product and second-level copy. "AFL" is retired.

**What Adray Core is not:** a replacement for Meta Ads Manager, another dashboard to babysit, a social media tool, or an ads-only tool. It looks at the whole business: ads, site, products, inventory and sales.
